Direct Preference Optimization. Your Language Model is Secretly a Reward Model.md
Abstract. Teach a language model what people prefer with one ordinary training loss, instead of a reward model followed by reinforcement learning.

Overview

Setting
Making a model’s answers match what people prefer (which summary or reply is better), given pairs of answers where a person picked one.
Motivation
The usual route, RLHF, first fits a reward model to those choices and then tunes the language model against it with reinforcement learning, which is costly and unstable. Key insight: a reward and the best policy for it determine each other, so the reward can be written in terms of the model itself.
Method
  1. Take a prompt with two answers, one of them preferred.
  2. Measure how much more likely the tuned model makes each answer than a frozen copy of the original model does.
  3. Raise that gain for the preferred answer and lower it for the other.
Why it works
Implicit reward: the model’s own probabilities play the part of the reward, so there is no second model to train and keep in step.
