Design choices were measured

Each converter, prompt and choice of model was tested on real papers before it was adopted. Six of the 22 experiments:
Question
Measured
Decision
Convert from arXiv’s HTML or from the PDF?(experiments/pdf-vs-arxiv-html)
The HTML kept 35 of 35 equations as exact LaTeX. A layout-aware PDF tool kept 1 and dropped the rest without a trace.
HTML first; the PDF only when there is no HTML
Which converter for papers that are only a PDF?(experiments/pdf-converter-bakeoff)
Four converters on six papers. docling with surya put 100% of table numbers in tables and read 71% of equations closely; marker alone, 83% and 68%.
docling for layout and tables, surya to read each equation
Keep bold and underline?(experiments/table-emphasis)
They mark a table’s best result. Keeping them restored 9,930 marks in 91 papers and made the papers 0.6% longer.
Kept: cheap, and the agent needs it
Keep the shading of table cells?(experiments/table-emphasis)
Questions answered correctly: 11 of 15 without it, 15 of 15 with it. Three of the four gains are stated in the paper’s text anyway.
Kept only where the caption refers to it
Which model reads a paper into its note?(experiments/reading-models)
Five models were tested as the reader on the same six papers. Errors found when their notes were checked against the papers: Opus 1, Sonnet 6, Gemini Pro 7, Gemini Flash 9, Haiku 35.
Opus reads, one request a paper
Answer from the review, or from the papers?(experiments/review-usefulness)
Starting from the review, then checking the paper: 4.7 wrong statements over three questions and 22% fewer tokens. Without reviews: 6.3 wrong; without reviews or notes: 3.3. The answers were no more complete.
Start from the review, and check the paper before stating a number
