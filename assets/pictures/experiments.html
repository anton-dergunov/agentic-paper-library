Design choices were measured

Each converter, prompt and choice of model was tested on real papers before it was adopted. Six of the 22 experiments:
Question
Measured
Decision
Convert from arXiv’s HTML or from the PDF?
The HTML kept 35 of 35 equations as exact LaTeX. A layout-aware PDF tool kept 1 and dropped the rest without a trace.
HTML first; the PDF only when there is no HTML
Which converter for papers that are only a PDF?
Four compared on six papers. The best pairing put 100% of table numbers in tables and read 71% of equations closely.
A layout model, plus a model that reads each equation
Keep bold and underline?
They mark a table’s best result. Keeping them restored 9,930 marks in 91 papers and made the papers 0.6% longer.
Kept: cheap, and the agent needs it
Keep the shading of table cells?
Questions answered correctly: 11 of 15 without it, 15 of 15 with it. Three of the four gains are stated in the paper’s text anyway.
Kept only where the caption refers to it
Which model reads a paper into its note?
Errors in six notes, for five models: 1, 6, 7, 9 and 35.
The most accurate one, in one request a paper
Answer from the review, or from the papers?
From the review alone: a third of the tokens, but 8 wrong statements against 3.
The review guides the search; the answer is checked in the paper
22 experiments are in the repository, each with its method, scripts and results. Used by the author on a library of about 2,200 papers.
