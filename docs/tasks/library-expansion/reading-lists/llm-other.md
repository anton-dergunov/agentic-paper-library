# Reading list: LLM, other folders

The library's remaining LLM folders are strong on 2025-2026 specifics but thin on the classics and the standard references. It holds Llama 3, DeepSeek-V3/V3.2, Qwen3, GLM-4.5, Kimi K2 and gpt-oss as model reports; Mamba, RWKV, xLSTM, Titans and Gated DeltaNet on the recurrent side; a deep stock of agent-memory, personalization and memory-benchmark papers (mostly 2025-2026); ReAct, Reflexion, Toolformer and AlphaEvolve for agents; Self-RAG, GraphRAG, Ragas and CRAG for RAG; and a handful of quantization, pruning and routing papers. What is missing is the foundations a practitioner is expected to know: chain-of-thought prompting and its descendants (self-consistency, tree of thoughts, program-aided reasoning), the original scaling laws and open pretraining corpora, test-time compute scaling, the pre-2024 retrieval-augmented language models (REALM, FiD, RETRO, Atlas), the standard jailbreak and prompt-injection attacks, confidence calibration and hallucination detection, speculative decoding and the main quantization methods. The list adds those, plus the model reports that set the 2024-2026 standard (DeepSeek-V4, GLM-5, Kimi K2.5, Nemotron 3, MiniMax-01, LongCat-Flash, Gemma 3, Gemini 1.5/2.5, Phi-4), the efficient-attention lineage behind them (GQA, YaRN, StreamingLLM, NSA, MoBA, gated attention, Kimi Linear, Mamba-3), the deep-research and agentic-RL references, and the alignment-faking, emergent-misalignment and persona-vector line for the behaviour folder. Folders that are already well stocked (agent memory, memory benchmarks, personalization benchmarks, text analytics) get only a few real gaps. llm/evaluation and llm/post-training are covered by their own lists. Scaling Laws for Precision, Scaling Laws and Compute-Optimal Training Beyond Fixed Training Durations and Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think are proposed in the deep-learning and interpretability lists and are left out here. The original DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi K1.5 reports belong to the post-training list.
