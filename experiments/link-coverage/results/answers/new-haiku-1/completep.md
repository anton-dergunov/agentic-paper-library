**Main idea:** the paper proposes **CompleteP**, a rule for scaling a transformer's hyperparameters as it gets deeper and wider, so that a learning rate tuned on a small model still works on a deep one, and every layer keeps learning real features.

The problem it targets is that existing depth-scaling rules fail in one of two ways. Some don't let the optimal learning rate transfer across depth, so you either re-tune at each size or train sub-optimally. Others transfer the learning rate but leave layers in a "lazy" regime, where each layer learns only features close to its linearization and the extra depth adds little nonlinearity ([abstract, p. 1](http://pdf.invalid/deep-learning/optimizers-and-schedules/Don't%20be%20lazy.%20CompleteP%20enables%20compute-efficient%20deep%20transformers.pdf?page=1)).

The mechanism is one change to how each residual block's output is scaled by depth L. The block output is multiplied by L^(−α), with α between 0.5 and 1 ([Eq. 1, p. 2](http://pdf.invalid/deep-learning/optimizers-and-schedules/Don't%20be%20lazy.%20CompleteP%20enables%20compute-efficient%20deep%20transformers.pdf?page=2)). Prior work favoured α = 0.5, and the authors find α = 1 is the unique choice that gives both depth-wise transfer and complete feature learning. They call that CompleteP. To make it work they also rescale LayerNorm, the bias learning rates, AdamW's weight decay, and AdamW's ε ([p. 2](http://pdf.invalid/deep-learning/optimizers-and-schedules/Don't%20be%20lazy.%20CompleteP%20enables%20compute-efficient%20deep%20transformers.pdf?page=2)).

The payoff is compute. At 1.9B parameters, α = 1 saves 11.8% FLOPs over µP for compute-optimally shaped models and 34.4% for a 179-layer model. It also keeps a wider range of width-to-depth ratios compute-efficient ([p. 2](http://pdf.invalid/deep-learning/optimizers-and-schedules/Don't%20be%20lazy.%20CompleteP%20enables%20compute-efficient%20deep%20transformers.pdf?page=2)).
