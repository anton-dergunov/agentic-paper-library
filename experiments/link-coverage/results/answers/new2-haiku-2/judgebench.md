**Short answer:** read §3 (pp. 4–6) and the scoring rule in §4 (p. 6). Those two sections hold the whole method. Then check the appendix counts (A.3) and the generator ablation (§4.4, p. 10). Skip the judge details and prompts unless you plan to rerun it.

**What to read, in order**

1. **Idea and pipeline, p. 4–5** ([§3, p. 4](http://pdf.invalid/llm/evaluation/methods/JudgeBench.%20A%20Benchmark%20for%20Evaluating%20LLM-based%20Judges.pdf?page=4), [Figure 2, p. 4](http://pdf.invalid/llm/evaluation/methods/JudgeBench.%20A%20Benchmark%20for%20Evaluating%20LLM-based%20Judges.pdf?page=4)). Take a dataset with ground truth and an automatic checker. Sample k answers from one strong model (GPT-4o). Keep only questions with at least one correct and one incorrect answer, then pair a correct with an incorrect one. The key claim is on p. 5: a model that answers a question inconsistently also can't tell right from wrong on it.
2. **Sources and filtering, p. 5–6** ([p. 5](http://pdf.invalid/llm/evaluation/methods/JudgeBench.%20A%20Benchmark%20for%20Evaluating%20LLM-based%20Judges.pdf?page=5), [p. 6](http://pdf.invalid/llm/evaluation/methods/JudgeBench.%20A%20Benchmark%20for%20Evaluating%20LLM-based%20Judges.pdf?page=6)). MMLU-Pro, LiveBench and LiveCodeBench supply the questions. GPT-4o-mini is used as a second checker, and responses where it disagrees with the regex checker are dropped (format errors).
3. **Scoring, p. 6** ([§4, p. 6](http://pdf.invalid/llm/evaluation/methods/JudgeBench.%20A%20Benchmark%20for%20Evaluating%20LLM-based%20Judges.pdf?page=6)). Each pair is judged in both orders. A pair counts as correct only if the two verdicts agree on the right answer. Inconsistent verdicts count as wrong, so a coin-flip judge scores below 50%.
4. **Dataset counts, A.3, p. 19** ([p. 19](http://pdf.invalid/llm/evaluation/methods/JudgeBench.%20A%20Benchmark%20for%20Evaluating%20LLM-based%20Judges.pdf?page=19)). This explains the subsampling and the augmented-set check (Table 8: the judge ranking is unchanged at 770 knowledge pairs).
5. **Generator ablation, §4.4, p. 10** ([Figure 4 and text, p. 10](http://pdf.invalid/llm/evaluation/methods/JudgeBench.%20A%20Benchmark%20for%20Evaluating%20LLM-based%20Judges.pdf?page=10)). Claude-generated pairs (270) drop Claude's judge from 64.3% to 44.8%. This is a selection effect: the questions were kept because Claude is unstable on them. It is not self-preference.

**Most useful overall:** §3, the §4 scoring rule, and the §4.4 caveat. Together they tell you what the benchmark measures and what its 50% baseline means. A.4 (length bias, p. 19) is a short check worth a glance. A.5 (checker agreement, pp. 19–21) is only needed if you rebuild the data. A.1, A.2 and A.6 cover the fine-tuned judges and prompts.

**Inconsistency to know about:** the main text (p. 6) gives 56 math pairs, for a total of 350. A.3 (p. 19) gives 46 math pairs from GPT-4o. The appendix numbers add up to 340, not 350, so the body and appendix disagree. I'd cite the appendix breakdown only with that caveat. Our note's digest repeats the body's 56, so it inherits the problem.
