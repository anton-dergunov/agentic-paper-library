## Digest

- Mechanism: phi-3's architecture is kept almost unchanged, and the gains are attributed to data, curriculum and post-training. The architecture is a decoder-only 14B model with a 4096 context, extended to 16K in midtraining. It switches to the tiktoken tokenizer (padded vocab 100,352) and uses full attention over 4K instead of phi-3-medium's 2K sliding window (p. 7).
- Training setup disclosed: ~10T tokens, peak learning rate 0.0003, weight decay 0.1, global batch 5760 (p. 7). Midtraining uses 250B tokens, rope base frequency 250K, a learning rate 10x lower than in pretraining, and a mix of 30% new long-context data and 70% recall tokens (p. 10).
- Synthetic data: 50 broad dataset types, ~400B unweighted tokens, built with seed curation, rewrite and augment, self-revision, instruction reversal for code, and execution-based validation (p. 5). Question seeds are filtered by majority voting, discarding questions where all answers agree or none do. Plurality answers stand in for ground truth in rejection sampling (p. 5).
- Organic data: filtering uses small non-LLM classifiers trained on ~10^6 LLM annotations, plus custom extractors and multilingual data (p. 6). The paper says organic questions beat synthetic ones in ablations, and that rewritten questions gave smaller gains, but gives no numbers (p. 6).
- Final pretraining mix (p. 9, Table 5): web 15% (1.3T unique tokens, 1.2 epochs), web rewrites 15% (290B, 5.2), synthetic 40% (290B, 13.8), code 20% (820B, 2.4), acquired sources 10% (580B, 1.7).
- Mix ablations were done at 1T tokens and 7B scale and transferred to 14B on a rank-correlation argument (p. 9). In Table 4, the synthetic-heavy variant S scores an average +0.8 relative to the final mix and S+WR +0.4. The final mix was kept for knowledge benchmarks, and the paper says the gap largely closes after post-training, with no numbers given (p. 9, Table 4).
- Synthetic-only ablation: a 13B model trained only on synthetic data (20+ repeats per source) is +12.1 on HumanEval but -14.8 on TQA relative to phi-3-medium. Adding web rewrites gives -7.7 on TQA (p. 8, Table 3). More epochs of synthetic data beat fresh web tokens on MMLU at small scale (p. 8, Figure 2, values not visible).
- Pretrained phi-4 (4k) versus phi-3-medium: MMLU pro +10.3, MATH +8.9, HumanEval +7.8, TQA -0.7. These use an internal log-likelihood/few-shot harness (p. 7, Table 2).
- Long context (HELMET, p. 10, Table 6): phi-4 at 16K scores Recall 99.0, RAG 57.1, ICL 77.0, Re-rank 54.4, QA 36.0, Summ 40.5. GPT-4o at 16K scores 100.0, 66.7, 85.6, 73.8, 43.7, 46.3. Qwen-2.5-14B at 16K scores Re-rank 50.3 and ICL 67.6. The paper does not compare against phi-3.
- Post-training (p. 12): one SFT round (~8B tokens, learning rate 10^-6, 40 languages), then pivotal-token DPO, then judge-guided DPO. The second DPO round uses ~850k pairs, with responses from GPT-4o, GPT-4t and phi-4 and GPT-4o as judge. The pair counts are 266,000 any-vs-any overall, 532,000 accuracy, and 43,842 unknown+safety (p. 12, Table 8).
- Pivotal Token Search (PTS, p. 14): it recursively subdivides a response, estimating p(success) by sampling and an oracle (tests or ground-truth answers), to find single tokens that move success probability by at least p_gap. The accepted and rejected tokens form a one-token DPO pair. Questions are filtered to 0.2 ≤ p(success) ≤ 0.8 (p. 14). The paper admits it may miss some pivotal tokens. Its example in Figure 3 uses GPT-4o with an initial success probability of 0.31 (N=529), not phi-4. PTS pair counts are given in Table 7, and no hyperparameter value for p_gap is given.
- Post-training ablation (p. 16, Table 9), SFT → DPO stage 1 → DPO stage 2 only → both: GPQA 47.3 → 53.6 → 52.4 → 56.1. MATH goes 77.1 → 80.5 → 77.6 → 80.4. ArenaHard goes 56.7 → 66.5 → 69.8 → 75.4. IFEval goes 66.2 → 63.0 → 63.0 → 63.0, so instruction following declines after SFT. DROP goes 82.8 → 86.1 → 71.8 → 75.5. Each configuration is a single run, with no variance.
- Headline (p. 1, Table 1, simple-evals at temperature 0.5): phi-4 scores GPQA 56.1 versus GPT-4o 50.6 (Qwen-2.5-14B 42.9, phi-3 31.2). MATH is 80.4 versus GPT-4o 74.6 (Qwen-2.5-14B 75.6, phi-3 44.6). MMLU is 84.8 versus GPT-4o 88.1. HumanEval is 82.6 versus GPT-4o 90.6. The "surpasses its teacher" claim rests on GPQA and MATH only. The paper never says which model generated which synthetic data, so "teacher = GPT-4o" is asserted, not shown.
- Weaknesses the paper concedes (p. 1, Table 1; p. 20): SimpleQA 3.0 versus GPT-4o 39.4 and Qwen-2.5-14B 5.4; IFEval 63.0 versus Qwen-2.5-14B 78.7 and Llama-3.3-70B 89.3; DROP 75.5 versus Qwen-2.5-14B 85.5. The SimpleQA drop is framed as intentional refusal behaviour, supported by Figure 6, which is not visible (p. 16). It also reports factual hallucination, verbosity, and a 9.9 vs 9.11 error.
- Benchmark caveat on Table 1 (p. 1): Llama-3.3 MATH is 66.3 here, while Meta reports 77, and its HumanEval is 78.9 versus Meta's 88. The authors attribute this to simple-evals' strict formatting. Four of the 12 benchmarks use an internal framework. PhiBench, the internal benchmark written by the authors, was used to choose data mixtures and hyperparameters, and phi-4 scores 56.2 on it versus GPT-4o 72.4 (p. 16; p. 1, Table 1). So it is not independent evidence.
- Contamination evidence (p. 3): a fresh test on the Nov 2024 AMC-10/12 (four tests, max 150, 100 runs at temperature 0.5, ±2σ error bars), run only after the hyperparameters were fixed. The paper says phi-4 beats larger frontier models, but the numerical scores are in Figure 1 and are not in the text. QwQ-32B-Preview averages 124.5 and uses 4X more tokens (p. 3). The n-gram decontamination method is deferred to Appendix B, and the authors concede it does not catch rephrasing (p. 16).
- Safety (p. 19, Table 10): GPT-4o simulates the conversations and scores them. phi-4 has Jailbreak DR1 0.073 versus phi-3 14B 0.111 and 3P Content Harms DR1 0.121 versus 0.251, but Harmful Content Continuation DR3 0.036 versus 0.01 for phi-3 14B, which is worse. Baselines are mostly 7-8B models. The Microsoft AI Red Team ran a two-week exercise and found behaviour similar to phi-3. GCG suffixes generated on phi-3-medium did not transfer (p. 19).
- Not disclosed: the data sources and mix beyond the categories above, generator model identities, the code and data themselves, compute, training-run counts or seeds, the SFT mix composition, and decontamination details (all appendices are missing from the text).

## Related

- phi-3 family [1], [10], [14], [18]: same architecture with a small change (full attention, tiktoken); the improvements come from the synthetic-data share and from post-training; compared on Tables 1-3.
- GPT-4o, the claimed teacher: phi-4 exceeds it on GPQA and MATH but trails it on most other benchmarks and by a wide margin on SimpleQA.
- Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct: the main same-size comparator; phi-4 wins 9 of 12 benchmarks, loses on SimpleQA, DROP and IFEval (p. 18).
- Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B: larger open models that phi-4 meets or exceeds on reasoning and coding but not on IFEval or SimpleQA.
- QwQ-32B, o1, DeepSeek-R1-Lite [9], [23], [28]: long-chain-of-thought models, argued to be in a different cost class; compared only on AMC.
- DPO [26]: the preference-optimisation base, with PTS as a token-level pair generator.
- [20] contrastive token weighting for DPO, and [33], [19] process supervision: PTS estimates p(success) directly by sampling instead of with a learned proxy or process reward model.
- HELMET [35], simple-evals [24], GPQA [25]: evaluation tools the report relies on; GPQA is cited as contamination-resistant.
