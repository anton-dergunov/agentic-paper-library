## Digest

- Gemma 3 adds vision, 128K context (32K for 1B) and wider multilingual coverage to Gemma 2. Sizes are 1B, 4B, 12B and 27B. It is a dense decoder-only transformer with GQA, pre-norm and post-norm RMSNorm, and QK-norm replacing Gemma 2's soft-capping (p. 1).
- Architecture change for long context: 5 local sliding-window layers per 1 global layer, starting with a local layer, with a local span of 1024 tokens (p. 1). Gemma 2 used 1:1 (p. 6).
- Global-layer RoPE base frequency goes from 10k to 1M, local layers stay at 10k (p. 1). Models are pre-trained at 32K sequences, then extended to 128K at the end of pre-training with RoPE rescaling, scaling factor 8 (p. 7). The paper says models generalize to 128K and degrade rapidly beyond that (p. 7, Fig. 7). It gives no long-context task benchmark in the main text.
- KV-cache ablation, a 2B text-only model at 32k context: "global only" has a memory overhead of 60%, against less than 15% for 1:3 with sw=1024 (p. 6, Fig. 5). This is read from the text because the figure is missing. The same section reports "minimal impact" on perplexity from the local:global ratio, even at 7:1 (p. 6, Fig. 3), and from shrinking the window (p. 6, Fig. 4). Only perplexity was measured, with no downstream long-context quality.
- Vision: a 400M SigLIP variant at 896×896, shared and frozen across the 4B, 12B and 27B models, giving 256 soft tokens per image. Embeddings are pre-computed, so there is no added training cost (p. 2, p. 3). The 1B model has no vision encoder (p. 2, Table 1). Pan & Scan is inference-time only crop windowing for non-square or high-resolution images (p. 2).
- Resolution ablation, a short-schedule 2B model: DocVQA 31.9 / 59.8, InfoVQA 23.1 / 33.7 and TextVQA 44.1 / 58.0 at 256 vs 896 resolution (p. 8, Table 7). P&S, 4-shot, versus no P&S: 27B DocVQA 85.6 to 90.4, InfoVQA 59.4 to 76.4, TextVQA 68.6 to 70.2; 4B DocVQA 72.8 to 81.0 (p. 8, Table 8). The text says "27B IT", while the table caption says a pre-trained checkpoint (inconsistent).
- Pre-training: distillation from an undisclosed teacher, sampling 256 logits per token weighted by teacher probabilities, with the rest zeroed and renormalized (p. 2). Token budgets are 14T (27B), 12T (12B), 4T (4B) and 2T (1B) (p. 2). The 256k vs 262k vocabulary figures conflict (p. 2, Table 1). The tokenizer is the Gemini 2.0 SentencePiece one (p. 2).
- Data disclosed only qualitatively: more multilingual data, both monolingual and parallel, with a rebalancing strategy inspired by Chung et al. (2023); safety and PII filtering; decontamination; quality reweighting (p. 2). No mixture proportions, image data sources or teacher identity.
- Distillation ablation: for short training horizons a smaller teacher is better, and the trend reverses for longer training. Only a relative perplexity figure is given (p. 7, Fig. 8). Teacher sizes and horizons are not stated in the text.
- Post-training: distillation from a large IT teacher plus RL with improved BOND, WARM and WARP. Rewards come from weight-averaged reward models trained on human feedback, code execution feedback and ground-truth math rewards (p. 4). Data filtering covers personal information, unsafe outputs, wrong self-identification and duplicates (p. 4). Details of data, reward models, hyperparameters and "improved" variants are not disclosed. There is no ablation of post-training components, though the abstract calls the recipe "novel".
- QAT: about 5,000 steps of fine-tuning with the non-quantized checkpoint's probabilities as targets, in per-channel int4, per-block int4 and switched fp8 (p. 3). For 27B, bf16 weights are 54.0 GB against 14.1 GB for Int4; with the KV cache at 32k they are 72.7 GB and 32.8 GB (p. 3, Table 3). No quality numbers for the quantized models.
- Training infrastructure: TPUv5e with 512 chips for 1B and 2048 for 4B, TPUv4 with 6144 chips for 12B, TPUv5p with 6144 chips for 27B (p. 3, Table 2).
- Main IT results, Gemma 3 27B vs Gemma 2 27B vs Gemini 1.5 Pro, in that order: MMLU-Pro 67.5 / 56.9 / 75.8; LiveCodeBench 29.7 / 20.4 / 34.2; MATH 89.0 / 55.6 / 86.5; GPQA Diamond 42.4 / 34.3 / 59.1; SimpleQA 10.0 / 9.2 / 24.9; FACTS Grounding 74.9 / 62.4 / 80.0; Global MMLU-Lite 75.1 / 68.6 / 80.8; HiddenMath 60.3 / 14.8 / 52.0; MMMU (val) 64.9 / – / 65.9 (p. 4, Table 6). Gemma 3 27B beats Gemini 1.5 Pro only on MATH and HiddenMath, ties on Bird-SQL at 54.4 / 54.4, and trails on the rest. The abstract's "comparable to Gemini-1.5-Pro across benchmarks" is therefore generous. Gemma 3 4B vs Gemma 2 27B: MATH 75.6 vs 55.6, MMLU-Pro 43.6 vs 56.9, which only partly supports "4B-IT competitive with 27B-IT".
- Chatbot Arena: Gemma-3-27B-IT 1338 (+8/-9), rank 9, against DeepSeek-V3 1318, Llama-3.1-405B 1269, Qwen2.5-72B 1257 and Gemma-2-27B-it 1220 (p. 4, Table 5). The score is a preliminary result from March 8, 2025. The paper notes that Elo ignores vision. The top models are Grok-3-Preview 1412 and GPT-4.5-Preview 1411. The text says "top 10", which is by rank, with the confidence intervals overlapping neighbours.
- Evaluation stance: the paper declines direct comparison with external open models on static benchmarks because "running them in our setting does not guarantee a fair comparison", and defers to third-party leaderboards (p. 5). The appendix of extra benchmarks is not in the supplied text. The prompting setup, sampling and shot counts for Table 6 beyond "zero-shot" are not given. The paper notes the risk of contamination of the pre-training probes despite decontamination (p. 5).
- Pre-training probing (Fig. 2) claims improvements "in most categories" over Gemma 2 across science, code, factuality, multilinguality, reasoning and vision. Numbers are in the appendix, which is not supplied (p. 5).
- Memorization: exact and approximate (edit distance 10%) discoverable extraction with a 50-token prefix and a 50-token suffix. Gemma 3 memorizes long-form text at a much lower rate than prior Gemma and Gemini models, with approximate memorization about 24x exact on average, a relative increase (p. 8–9, Fig. 9). No personal information was observed in memorized outputs, using SDP, which is high-recall (p. 9). The rates themselves are in a missing figure.
- Safety: policy-violation rate on synthetic adversarial queries labelled by human raters is called "significantly low" with no numbers. CBRN knowledge is called "low", from an internal multiple-choice set plus a published chemical hazards set. Dangerous-capability evaluations are streamlined, justified by the claim that a more capable model's evaluation is sufficient for less capable ones (p. 10). The release is judged to have "negligible effect on the overall risk landscape" (p. 9).
- Disclosed vs not: disclosed are the layer pattern, window, RoPE, parameter counts, token counts, chip counts, tokenizer and distillation sampling method. Not disclosed are the data mixture, the teacher, the post-training data and reward models, the learning-rate and batch settings, the compute in FLOPs, and the code to reproduce.

## Related

- Gemma 2 (Gemma Team, 2024b): direct predecessor; Gemma 3 changes the 1:1 local/global ratio to 5:1, drops soft-capping for QK-norm, and increases the token budgets; Gemma 2 is the main baseline in Table 6.
- Gemma 1 (Gemma Team, 2024a): earlier release; its governance approach and memorization methodology are reused.
- Gemini 1.5 and 2.0 (Gemini Team): co-designed family, the tokenizer is shared with Gemini 2.0, and these models are the only non-Gemma baselines in Table 6.
- SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023): vision encoder, a 400M variant used frozen with 896×896 input.
- LLaVA (Liu et al., 2024): inspiration for the flexible-resolution Pan & Scan method.
- Longformer (Beltagy et al., 2020) and Luong et al. (2015): sources for the sliding-window and global attention layers that are interleaved.
- Positional interpolation (Chen et al., 2023): basis for the RoPE rescaling used to extend context to 128K.
- BOND, WARM, WARP (Sessa et al. 2024; Ramé et al. 2024a, 2024b): RL components of post-training.
- DeepSeek-V3, Llama 3.1 405B, Qwen2.5-72B: larger open models that Gemma 3 27B is ranked against in Chatbot Arena only, not on benchmarks.
- Chatbot Arena (Chiang et al., 2024): the external human-preference evaluation.
