## Digest

- Model: 236B total parameters with 21B activated per token, 128K context (p. 1). 60 layers, hidden dimension 5120 (p. 12). Every FFN except the first layer's is MoE, with 2 shared and 160 routed experts, 6 routed experts activated per token, expert intermediate dimension 1536 (p. 12).
- MLA: keys and values are jointly compressed into one latent vector c^KV, and only the latent is cached (Eq. 9–11, p. 7). The up-projections are absorbed into W^Q and W^O at inference. Queries are also low-rank compressed, but only to save training activation memory (p. 7).
- Decoupled RoPE: RoPE is incompatible with the absorption trick, so extra RoPE queries and one shared RoPE key are used and the key is cached too (Eq. 14–19, p. 8). The cache is (d_c+d_h^R)l elements per token (p. 8, Table 1).
- MLA settings: n_h=128, d_h=128, d_c=512, d_c'=1536, d_h^R=64 (p. 12). Table 1 puts the cache at about 9/2 d_h l, "equal to GQA with only 2.25 groups" (p. 8, Table 1). The "Capability" column of Table 1 (MHA Strong, MLA Stronger) is a qualitative label, and the supporting ablation is in Appendix D, which is not shown here.
- DeepSeekMoE routing (p. 9): shared experts are always on and routed experts use top-K softmax affinity. Device-limited routing caps each token at M devices; the paper says "when M≥3" it roughly matches unrestricted routing (p. 9), and training uses M=3 (p. 12). No comparison numbers are given in this text.
- Balance: three auxiliary losses (expert-, device- and communication-level) with α1=0.003, α2=0.05, α3=0.02 (p. 10, p. 12). Device-level token dropping uses capacity factor 1.0, with about 10% of training sequences never dropped. Tokens are dropped in training but not in evaluation (p. 11, p. 12).
- Data: 8.1T tokens, Chinese tokens about 12% more than English, 100K BBPE vocabulary as in DeepSeek 67B (p. 11). Disclosed only qualitatively: more data, recovered over-filtered web data, a better quality filter, and removal of "contentious content". The corpus composition, sources and mixture are not given. Contamination handling is not described in the main text.
- Training recipe: AdamW with β1=0.9, β2=0.95, weight decay 0.1. Peak learning rate 2.4×10^-4, step decay ×0.316 at about 60% and 90% of tokens. Batch size ramps from 2304 to 9216 over the first 225B tokens. Sequence length 4K (p. 12). Trained on H800 GPUs with 16-way pipeline, 8-way expert and ZeRO-1 parallelism and no tensor parallelism (p. 12). Total GPU-hours and the training cost of the whole run are not given.
- Long context: YaRN with scale 40 applied to the decoupled key, then 1000 additional steps at 32K sequence length with batch size 576. The evaluation is a NIAH figure only, said to perform well up to 128K (p. 13, Fig. 4). There are no other long-context benchmarks.
- Efficiency claims against DeepSeek 67B (same family, internal): 300.6K versus 172.8K GPU hours per trillion tokens, stated as 42.5% savings (p. 16). KV cache reduced by 93.3% (abstract p. 1, p. 21); the main text gives no derivation, and the deployed model also uses FP8 weights and 6-bit average KV quantization (p. 16). Maximum generation throughput on 8 H800s is above 50K tokens/s, which is 5.76 times DeepSeek 67B, measured on the request distribution of DeepSeek's own deployed service (p. 16). The comparison mixes architecture with serving optimizations.
- Base results (p. 14, Table 2; all models run in the authors' framework, gaps under 0.3 treated as ties). Against DeepSeek 67B: MMLU 78.5 vs 71.3, GSM8K 79.2 vs 63.4, MATH 43.6 vs 18.7, HumanEval 48.8 vs 45.1. Against LLaMA3 70B: MMLU 78.5 vs 78.9, BBH 78.9 vs 81.0, GSM8K 79.2 vs 83.0, MATH 43.6 vs 42.2, C-Eval 81.7 vs 67.5, CMMLU 84.0 vs 69.3. The authors concede a "slight gap" in English. Qwen1.5 72B is higher on C-Eval (83.7 vs 81.7), CMMLU (84.3 vs 84.0) and AGIEval (64.4 vs 51.2). Mixtral 8x22B is higher on HumanEval (53.1 vs 48.8) and TriviaQA (82.1 vs 79.9). Qwen1.5 72B's CHID is blank because of a tokenizer error (p. 14, Table 2).
- SFT (p. 16): 1.5M instances (1.2M helpfulness, 0.3M safety), 2 epochs, learning rate 5×10^-6. The data itself is not released or described beyond domains. SFT lifts math and code over base (Table 3 vs Table 2): GSM8K 90.8 vs 79.2, MATH 52.7 vs 43.6. Table 3 is chat versus base and the few-shot formats differ, so treat it as indicative only. The paper states that no SFT data was seen in pre-training (p. 14).
- RL (p. 17–18): GRPO, which drops the critic and uses group-normalized rewards (Eq. 32–34). Two stages: reasoning alignment with a code/math reward model (compiler feedback, ground-truth labels), then preference alignment with a weighted sum of helpful, safety and rule-based rewards (Eq. 36). The coefficients c1–c3, ε, β, group size G, prompt counts and step counts are not given. Reward models are initialized from the SFT model. Infrastructure: hybrid engine, vLLM, CPU offloading.
- Chat results (p. 18, Table 3): RL over SFT gives HumanEval 81.1 vs 76.8, MATH 53.9 vs 52.7, LiveCodeBench 32.5 vs 28.7. It is slightly lower on MMLU (77.8 vs 78.4), BBH (79.7 vs 81.3) and C-Eval (78.0 vs 80.9). The paper names this "alignment tax" (p. 20). LLaMA3 70B Instruct is higher on MMLU (80.3) and IFEval (79.7, vs 63.8 for V2 RL). Table 3 notes that TriviaQA and NaturalQuestions may underestimate chat models that ignore few-shot formats.
- Open-ended (p. 18, Table 4): AlpacaEval 2.0 length-controlled win rate 38.9 for V2 RL vs 30.0 SFT, 34.4 LLaMA3 70B Instruct, 36.6 Qwen1.5 72B Chat, 16.6 DeepSeek 67B Chat. MT-Bench 8.97 vs 8.62 SFT and 8.95 LLaMA3 70B Instruct. The MT-Bench gap to LLaMA3 is 0.02 and no variance is reported. The text claims superiority over Qwen1.5 72B Chat on both benchmarks, but Table 4 shows Qwen at 8.61 vs the 8.62 SFT figure only, and the RL model's lead over Qwen on AlpacaEval is 2.3 points.
- AlignBench (p. 19, Table 5; judge GPT-4-0613, a possibly self-preferring family, though the judge is not discussed): overall 7.91 for V2 RL, vs 8.01 GPT-4-1106-Preview, 7.89 ERNIEBot-4.0-202404, 7.74 V2 SFT, 7.53 GPT-4-0613, 7.19 Qwen1.5-72B-Chat, 6.43 DeepSeek-67B-Chat. Reasoning is lower (7.45 vs 7.73 for GPT-4-1106-Preview). The paper's phrase "beats most closed-source models" (p. 4) rests on this Chinese-only table. Several competitors were re-evaluated by the authors through APIs (the starred rows).
- Discussion (p. 20–21): fewer than 10K SFT instances caused a "significant performance decline" on IFEval (no number given). Online RL "significantly outperforms" offline RL (no numbers or ablation shown).
- Disclosure gaps: no scaling-law or hyperparameter-search evidence, no data mixture, no total compute, no ablation of MLA or DeepSeekMoE in the main text, no seeds or confidence intervals, and no third-party replication within the paper. The "strongest open-source MoE" claim is relative to the comparison set (Mixtral 8x22B, with Qwen and LLaMA3 as dense baselines) and rests on the authors' own evaluation harness.
- Limitations stated (p. 21): no knowledge updates, hallucination, limited proficiency beyond Chinese and English, text only.

## Related

- DeepSeek 67B (DeepSeek-AI, 2024): previous dense release, used as the baseline for all efficiency claims, and the source of the data pipeline, tokenizer and eval protocol.
- DeepSeekMoE (Dai et al., 2024): provides the fine-grained plus shared-expert FFN design adopted here, with new device-limited routing and communication balance loss added.
- GQA (Ainslie et al., 2023) / MQA (Shazeer, 2019): the KV-cache reduction methods MLA is positioned against (cache comparison in Table 1).
- GShard (Lepikhin et al., 2021), Switch (Fedus et al., 2021): the conventional MoE and balance-loss baselines; the token dropping idea is credited to Riquelme et al. (2021).
- DeepSeekMath / GRPO (Shao et al., 2024): the source of the critic-free RL algorithm used here.
- YaRN (Peng et al., 2023): the long-context extension, adapted to the decoupled RoPE key.
- Mixtral 8x22B, LLaMA3 70B, Qwen1.5 72B: the open comparison models. They were rerun in the authors' own framework rather than taken from their reports.
- AlpacaEval 2.0, MT-Bench, AlignBench (Dubois et al., Zheng et al., Liu et al.): the LLM-judged open-ended benchmarks for chat quality.
