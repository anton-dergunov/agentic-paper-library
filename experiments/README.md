# Experiments

Spikes, benchmarks and measured iterations behind the engine's design and its skills, one directory each. Every directory holds its write-up (the question, the method and the numbers) and its apparatus where there is one. The document it serves keeps only the decision, with a link back here.

Most of these ran in agent sessions while the library was being built, from 29 September to 3 October 2026, on the author's library of up to 2,200 papers. They were written up afterwards from the session transcripts, the scripts kept in session scratchpads, and git history. Where a saved output allowed it, the scores were recomputed on 3 October; each README says what was recomputed, what was recovered from a transcript, and what was lost.

**Results are committed** when they hold no personal data, credentials or private paths and come to a few MB of text. Converted paper text is committed only for openly licensed papers (CC BY); for the others, the scripts, the scores and short quotations are. PDFs, page images and caches are never committed.

Nothing here is imported by the engine. The experiments on the markdown conversion, which the library used until 26 Oct 2026, were removed with it ([design log](../docs/design.md#decisions-log)); they are in the history at commit `6466cd2`, and the apparatus kept here ran against the engine of its time.

| [`review-usefulness/`](review-usefulness/README.md) | Do reviews and notes help an agent answer questions? | Measured 5 and 6 Oct 2026 in three copies of the library: sessions find both unprompted. Told that the review is a map, sessions check notes and papers (five questions, three sessions each): wrong statements level with the copies without reviews, answers no more complete and narrower where the review covers the question, a fifth fewer tokens. Answers from the papers alone were as good. Sonnet matches Opus on a detail of one paper and makes half the points on a synthesis. The rule stays | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |

A request is 9K tokens and 4 s, against 228K and 55 s for the skill in a session. `paperlib add` shipped with Sonnet | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |
| [`prompt-trim/`](prompt-trim/README.md) | How many tokens do the library's instructions cost, and can they be cut without losing what they say? | Measured 7 Oct 2026: the library's share of a session's start fell from 8.3K to 5.5K Opus tokens, every rule kept ([`rules.md`](prompt-trim/rules.md)); ten paired sessions processed 16% fewer tokens. Notes, answers, overviews, adds, a reorganisation and review steps as good before and after; $18.92 | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |
| [`paper-vetting/`](paper-vetting/README.md) | Does a first opinion on a paper agree with what the reader decided? | Measured 9 Oct 2026 on 72 papers (30 skipped after the reader's review, 42 kept): the verdict does not separate them. The skips were mostly relative to a reading list (overlap, superseded), a bar the request does not state. A Sonnet verdict is 11.3K tokens and $0.029; Haiku's cost $0.050. The recommendation was dropped: `paperlib info` reports and does not recommend | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |
| [`reading-formats/`](reading-formats/README.md) | What does the library's markdown buy over the PDF, its text layer and arXiv's HTML, in one request and for an agent? | Measured 24–26 Oct 2026 on 37 papers, 118 questions and 24 notes: one request with the PDF 51 of 51 single-paper questions, the text layer and the markdown 43, both losing only values drawn in plots; across papers an agent with text layers equal to one in the library and cheaper; with the PDF beside the text layer, an agent opens the page for 9 of 9 drawn values and none of the text questions, unprompted, and for 5 of 9 passages where the text layer is broken; notes from the text layer preferred in 9 of 12 blind comparisons, with no wrong page against 17. The text layer beside the PDF replaces the markdown as the agent's copy | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |
| [`web-articles/`](web-articles/README.md) | Which converter should make a web article's markdown? | Run 26 Oct 2026 on three of the library's 19 web articles, one per kind of site: Defuddle kept 94–99% of the old converter's sentences, every equation as TeX, the footnotes and the code; Readability + Turndown printed each formula twice and lost the code; Trafilatura dropped every heading and a third of the long article. Defuddle shipped, on the page as Chrome renders it, without images | [`docs/text-layer.md`](../docs/text-layer.md#web-articles) |
| [`reading-effort/`](reading-effort/README.md) | Does a lower effort level read a paper as well? | Measured 30 Oct 2026 on six papers: over six notes `high` made 2 errors, `--effort medium` 5 and `low` 8, and a blind judge kept the `high` note for all six. `medium` would save 28% a read, `low` 37%. `paperlib read` now passes `--effort high` itself instead of taking the user's setting; $3.17 | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |
| [`paper-type/`](paper-type/README.md) | Can a cheap request at filing find the type a whole-paper reading finds? | Measured 30 Oct 2026 on 105 papers, 15 a type: the filing request with a type field agreed with the note on 97, a type-only request with the stored summary on 100; read by hand, 3 and 1 were clearly wrong, the rest border papers (position against study or method, method against system). $0.0034 a type-only request, nothing extra at filing. Filing writes the type, `paperlib fill-types` backfills it, a reading replaces it; $0.96 | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |
| [`pdf-locations/`](pdf-locations/README.md) | Can a citation mark the exact place in the PDF, and will an agent write such links? | Measured 30 Oct 2026 on 59 citations from the reader's chats: `paperlib locate` (PyMuPDF 2.0) marks the right region for 58, the 59th a phrase that repeats on its page, which it reports; agents had given the wrong page for 7 of 21 tables, so the whole paper is searched after the page. The rule costs 95 tokens of the guide; $0.06 | [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |
| [`text-layer-switch/`](text-layer-switch/README.md) | What does moving a library of 2,275 papers from markdown to text layers change, and what shows only in use? | Done 26 Oct 2026: 2,256 text layers in 100 s, 19 web articles converted again, 2.6 GB of figures and 825 conversion records gone, every review's links repointed; the reading view's reference cut rebuilt (667 → 110 papers left whole, 5 min → 5 s); three notes read from text layers with exact pages; in the library, the agent opens the PDF page for a drawn value, but Claude Code refuses to read a PDF outside the library until the folder is allowed. Afterwards (30 Oct), the pages the kept notes and reviews cite, which the markdown's headings had set to their section's first page, were corrected from the old markdown left in git: 12,657 in notes and 595 in reviews, four in five prose moves a fix on 2,496 known answers, $2.80 of Haiku | [`docs/text-layer.md`](../docs/text-layer.md), [`docs/design.md`](../docs/design.md#decisions-log) |

| [`meaning-search/`](meaning-search/README.md) | Which local encoder, embedding which text, finds the papers a reader describes? | Measured 31 Oct 2026 on 2,275 papers, 32 reader-style queries with pooled top 10s graded by Sonnet: Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B reached nDCG@10 0.752, bge-base 0.619, MiniLM 0.574, BM25 0.504 and SPECTER 0.373; the abstract carries the meaning, the one-line summary alone does not. 65–175 ms a query on the M1.
