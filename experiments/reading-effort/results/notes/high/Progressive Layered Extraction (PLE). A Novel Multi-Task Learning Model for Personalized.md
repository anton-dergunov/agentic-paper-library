# Progressive Layered Extraction (PLE): A Novel Multi-Task Learning (MTL) Model for Personalized Recommendations

type: method
family: gated mixture-of-experts multi-task learning for recommendation ranking
evidence: Tencent News video logs (VTR/VCR, CTR/VCR, 3- and 4-task groups) against single-task, hard sharing, asymmetric sharing, cross-stitch, sluice, customized sharing, MMOE and ML-MMOE; 4-week online A/B test; synthetic data, census-income and Ali-CCP (public sets compared only with single-task and MMOE); strength: vendor-run, single runs with no variance or significance tests, the only ablation is CGC vs PLE, loss-weighting tricks not ablated

## Digest

- Claim: when task correlations are complex and depend on the sample, existing MTL models show a "seesaw phenomenon": one task improves while another gets worse, compared with the single-task model. PLE is meant to remove this and negative transfer (p. 1).
- CGC (Customized Gate Control) is the single-level version. Each task has its own experts, and there is a set of shared experts. Each task's tower reads only its own experts plus the shared ones. A per-task softmax gate over the input weights these experts: w_k(x)=Softmax(W_gk x), W_gk ∈ R^{(m_k+m_s)×d} (p. 5, Eqs. 2–5). Unlike MMOE, a task's tower has no connection to other tasks' specific experts.
- PLE stacks several CGC-style extraction layers and adds a gate for the shared experts that selects over all experts in that layer. Higher-level gates take the lower-level gate outputs as their selector, not the raw input. Parameters are therefore separated step by step ("progressive separation routing") (p. 6, Eqs. 6–7, Figure 5).
- Training: the loss covers the union of all tasks' sample spaces. Each task's loss is masked to its own sample space with an indicator δ_k^i (for example, share and comment only happen after a click) (p. 7, Eq. 9). Loss weights change over time: an initial weight times γ_k^t per epoch t (p. 7, Eq. 10). The equation text is garbled in extraction. The values of ω_k,0 and γ_k are not given, the effect of either trick is not ablated, and the paper does not say whether baselines also used them.
- MTL gain is defined as the MTL model's metric minus the single-task model's metric, with the sign flipped for MSE (p. 7, Eq. 11).
- Data: Tencent News logs from 8 consecutive days, with 46.926 million users, 2.682 million videos and 0.995 billion samples. The first 7 days are used for training (p. 7). The abstract says "1 billion samples" (p. 1).
- Setup: each task has a three-layer MLP [256, 128, 64] with ReLU, and experts are single-layer. All multi-level models have two levels. ML-MMOE is MMOE with multi-level experts and was built by the authors (p. 7). The tuned expert counts are not reported.
- VTR/VCR group (complex correlation), Table 1 (p. 7). Single-task has VTR AUC 0.6787 and VCR MSE 0.1321. MMOE gets 0.6803/0.1319, for gains of +0.0016/+0.0001. CGC gets 0.6832/0.1320, for +0.0045/+3.5E-5. PLE gets 0.6831/0.1307, for +0.0044/+0.0013. Sluice gets 0.6825 VTR but -0.0008 on VCR, and asymmetric sharing gets 0.6823 VTR but -0.0025 on VCR.
- The text says CGC and PLE "significantly outperform all baseline models in VTR" (p. 8). Table 1 shows CGC at 0.6832 against sluice at 0.6825, and no significance tests are reported. The paper also asserts that a 0.1% change in AUC or MSE matters online (p. 4), without data for it.
- CTR/VCR group (normal correlation), Table 2 (p. 8). Single-task has 0.7379 AUC and 0.1179 MSE. MMOE gets 0.7382/0.1175, for +0.0003/+0.0004. PLE gets 0.7406/0.1150, for +0.0027/+0.0029. CGC gets +0.0020/+0.0023. Cross-stitch drops CTR by -0.0158. Hard sharing, asymmetric sharing and customized sharing are left out of this table.
- Multiple tasks, Table 4 (p. 8), compares only CGC and PLE with single-task; no MTL baselines are shown. With all four tasks (VTR, VCR, SHR, CMR), PLE's gains are +0.0128/+0.0017/+0.0058/+0.0080 and CGC's are +0.0097/+0.0016/+0.0008/+0.0012. CGC's SHR gain is -0.0001 in the VTR+VCR+SHR group. The text says PLE beats CGC "significantly in all cases", yet in the four-task group VCR is +0.0017 vs +0.0016 and in the VTR+VCR+CMR group CMR is +0.0001 vs +0.0000.
- Online A/B test: VTR+VCR group, 4 weeks, users split into buckets. Table 3 reports gains over the single-task model (p. 8). PLE: +4.17% view count, +3.57% watch time. CGC: +3.92%/+2.75%. MMOE: +1.94%/+1.73%. ML-MMOE: +1.96%/+1.10%. Hard sharing: -1.65%/-1.79%. Bucket sizes and confidence intervals are not given.
- The abstract's "2.23% increase in view-count and 1.84% increase in watch time compared to SOTA MTL models" (p. 1; also p. 2) is not printed in Table 3, which only reports gains over single-task.
- Synthetic data follows MMOE's generator. The original hyperparameters were not available, so α_i and β_i were sampled from a standard normal, with c=1, m=10, d=512 and 1.4 million samples per correlation (p. 8). The paper reports that PLE gives an "87.2% increase in MTL gain over MMOE on average" (p. 9). Per-correlation results appear only in a plot (p. 9, Figure 7).
- Public sets, Table 5 (p. 10). Census-income has 299,285 samples and 40 features (p. 9). Task 2 AUCs are: single-task 0.9923, MMOE 0.9928, PLE 0.9945. Ali-CCP has 84 million samples (p. 9). Its CVR AUCs are: single-task 0.6040, MMOE 0.5738 (gain -0.0302), PLE 0.6097 (gain +0.0057). Its CTR AUCs are: single-task 0.6088, MMOE 0.6094, PLE 0.6112.
- Expert utilization (VTR/VCR group): CGC and PLE each had one expert per module, while MMOE and ML-MMOE had three per layer. According to the paper, CGC's two gates weight the experts very differently while MMOE's gates are similar, and MMOE never gives an expert zero weight. The paper reads this as evidence that MMOE "can hardly" converge to the CGC/PLE structure (p. 9, Figure 8). This evidence is qualitative and shown only in the plot. The seesaw picture itself is also shown only as a plot (p. 4, Figure 3).
- Not disclosed: the tuned expert counts and number of shared layers, the online combination weights w in Eq. 1 (p. 3), the loss-weight hyperparameters, the size of the online traffic, and any repeated runs.
- The paper's own inconsistencies: the text says "all models except cross-stitch exhibit positive MTL gain in both tasks", but ML-MMOE's CTR gain is -0.0001 (p. 8, Table 2). For census-income Task 1, MMOE's AUC of 0.9393 is below the single-task 0.9445, yet its MTL gain is printed as +0.0048 (p. 10, Table 5).

## Related in library

- Modeling Task Relationships in Multi-task Learning with Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts: MMOE is PLE's main baseline and starting point. PLE splits MMOE's shared experts into shared and task-specific ones, and borrows MMOE's synthetic-data setup and census-income task pair.
- Recommending What Video to Watch Next. A Multitask Ranking System: the paper cites this as the YouTube application of MMOE, which PLE is positioned against.
